# The Final Professional Trader Checklist

A professional does not rely on memory or mood in the moment of a trade; they rely on a checklist written when calm. This is the book distilled into one — a standing operating procedure to run before, during, and after every trade, and on a regular cycle. Copy it, adapt it to your own system, and honour it mechanically. The point of a checklist is to make your best thinking govern your worst moments.

*(Chapter references in parentheses point to the full treatment.)*

## A. Before the trading day (pre-market)

- [ ] **Verify I am fit to trade.** Rested, calm, not on tilt from a previous loss, not euphoric from a win (Ch 29). If not, stand down.
- [ ] **Check overnight and global cues, and the event calendar.** Any Budget, RBI policy, major results, or global trigger today or this week? (Ch 23) Size and structure accordingly.
- [ ] **Read the volatility regime.** India VIX level and IV Rank/Percentile — is volatility cheap, normal, or rich? (Ch 13, 14) This decides whether I should be a net buyer or seller of premium.
- [ ] **Confirm time-sensitive specifics.** Current lot sizes, expiry day (NIFTY Tuesday / SENSEX Thursday), margin rules, and any SEBI/exchange change (Ch 1, 2). Never trade on last year's specifications.
- [ ] **Review open positions' aggregate Greeks.** Net Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega for the whole book (Ch 12, 27). Are they within my target ranges?
- [ ] **Set the day's plan and limits.** Which playbook setups am I allowed to take, and what is today's **maximum loss limit** and **maximum number of trades**? (Ch 24, 29)

## B. Before entering any trade

- [ ] **Is this a playbook setup?** If it is not on my written playbook, I do not take it — no FOMO, no revenge, no "just this once" (Ch 28, 29).
- [ ] **State the thesis and the volatility view.** What must happen, by when, and what is my view on IV — not just direction (Ch 5, 14, 21).
- [ ] **Choose the structure to fit the view.** Direction × volatility → the right strategy; default to **defined risk** unless I have the capital and discipline for open-ended risk (Ch 21, 25).
- [ ] **Check the edge, not just the win rate.** Is expected value positive *after costs*? Breakeven win rate = 1/(1 + reward-to-risk) (Ch 21). A high win rate with a huge tail loss is not an edge.
- [ ] **Size the position by rule.** Risk ≤ 1–2% of capital per trade; lots = risk ÷ max loss per lot; fractional Kelly capped by the risk limit; size undefined risk off a **stress loss, never the premium** (Ch 26).
- [ ] **Price the costs.** Round-trip cost, spread, and STT — will the edge clear them? Scalp only tight-spread ATM strikes (Ch 3, 24).
- [ ] **Confirm liquidity.** Trade NIFTY/SENSEX, near the money, current expiry, with a tradable bid–ask (Ch 2, 3). Avoid stale, illiquid far strikes.
- [ ] **Pre-set the exit — before entry.** Profit target, stop-loss (prefer underlying-/thesis-based), and a time stop. Place the stop as a resting order or a mechanical rule, not a "mental stop" (Ch 25, 29).
- [ ] **Check margin and buffer.** Do I have the margin *with a 30–40% buffer* for an intraday volatility spike? Never run above ~60% utilisation (Ch 27).
- [ ] **Check portfolio impact.** How does this trade change my aggregate Greeks and my correlated exposure? All NIFTY positions move together (Ch 12, 25).

## C. While the trade is open

- [ ] **Monitor aggregate Greeks and alerts, not every tick** (Ch 12, 27). Manage the book, not the leg.
- [ ] **Honour the stop and the plan mechanically.** No widening stops, no "it'll come back," no removing hedges (Ch 25, 29).
- [ ] **Adjust only when objectively sound** — when the view is unchanged *and* the adjustment genuinely improves the position — never merely to avoid realising a loss. Respect the **rolling limit** (Ch 17, 18, 25).
- [ ] **Sequence rolls close-then-open** to avoid a transient peak-margin spike (Ch 27).
- [ ] **Watch expiry-day Gamma.** Near expiry, ATM Gamma explodes; a small move can flip your Delta and your P&L (Ch 9, 22).
- [ ] **Stop for the day if the daily loss limit is hit.** No exceptions. The trades taken after a painful loss are the account-killers (Ch 24, 29).

## D. Squaring off / at expiry

- [ ] **Square off intraday trades before the close** — do not let an intraday position become an un-hedgeable overnight gap (Ch 23, 24).
- [ ] **For ITM index options, square off to capture residual time value and control the exit price** — the old STT reason has gone (sale and exercise STT are equal at 0.15%), but the settlement-average and time-value reasons remain (Ch 2).
- [ ] **Do not rely on broker auto-square-off** as a risk plan; manage your own exit (Ch 24).

## E. After the trade / end of day (post-market)

- [ ] **Journal every trade by process, not outcome.** Was it a valid setup, correctly sized, with a pre-set stop, executed per the plan? A disciplined loss is a *good* trade; a lucky win is a *bad* one (Ch 28, 29).
- [ ] **Record the numbers.** Entry/exit, P&L, R-multiple, costs, and the Greeks at entry (Ch 28).
- [ ] **Update the equity curve and reconcile Greeks** with the broker's (Ch 27, 28).
- [ ] **Set aside tax.** Provision ~a third of net trading profit at the **marginal** rate; keep the advance-tax instalment funded (Ch 30).

## F. Weekly / monthly / quarterly review

- [ ] **Stress-test the book.** What happens to everything if NIFTY drops 10% overnight and VIX doubles? If the answer is ruin, cut size now (Ch 25).
- [ ] **Check portfolio heat and drawdown.** Total risk deployed as a fraction of capital; distance from my drawdown circuit-breaker. Respect the recovery asymmetry — avoiding the 50%+ drawdown matters more than the last few percent of return (Ch 25, 26).
- [ ] **Review the journal for behavioural patterns.** Which biases recur in my losses? Add a rule for each (Ch 29).
- [ ] **Review the system's live edge versus its backtest.** Is the real, deflated edge holding up? Scale size only as live results confirm it (Ch 28, 26).
- [ ] **Re-verify the market specifics.** Lot sizes, expiry days, margins, STT, tax slabs — anything changed? (Ch 1, 30)
- [ ] **File on time.** Especially in a loss year — a late return forfeits the eight-year loss carry-forward (Ch 30).

## G. The ten commandments (the whole book in ten lines)

1. **Survival first.** You cannot compound if you are out of the game (Ch 25).
2. **Costs and risk beat signals.** Optimise the variables you control (Ch 3, 25, 26).
3. **Default to defined risk.** Never take open-ended risk you cannot survive (Ch 17, 25).
4. **Size small, by rule.** Risk of ruin falls exponentially as per-trade risk shrinks (Ch 26).
5. **Keep a margin buffer.** Margin rises exactly when you most want to hold (Ch 27).
6. **Trade volatility and time, not just direction.** Options are positions in the Greeks (Ch 8–15).
7. **Sell volatility only when it is rich, and buy it only when it is cheap.** Respect the variance risk premium — indiscriminate selling is a net loser (Ch 14).
8. **Pre-set every exit; honour every stop.** You cannot out-willpower loss aversion in the moment (Ch 29).
9. **Judge process, not outcome; journal everything** (Ch 28, 29).
10. **Run it as a business** — lawful, taxed, adequately capitalised, with a three-year runway before you go full-time (Ch 30).

Print this. Read Section A every morning, Section B before every trade, and Section G whenever the market tempts you to abandon the rest.
