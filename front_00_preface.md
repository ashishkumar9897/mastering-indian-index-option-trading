# Preface

Most books on options were not written for the Indian market, and it shows. They price American-style options that can be exercised any day, when every index option on the NSE and BSE is European and cash-settled. They quote dollar premiums and S&P levels, when your screen shows NIFTY at 24,600 and a lot of 65. They ignore Securities Transaction Tax, the peak-margin snapshot, the one-weekly-expiry-per-exchange rule, and the particular way an Indian expiry day behaves — the very details that decide whether a real trade in Mumbai makes or loses money. This book was written to close that gap: to teach index option trading *as it is actually practised in India*, with the instruments, the microstructure, the taxes, and the regulations you will really meet.

It was also written against a sobering fact. SEBI's own studies have found, year after year, that the great majority of individual traders in the equity-derivatives segment **lose money** — around nine in ten, with aggregate losses running into lakhs of crores. That statistic is not a reason to stay away; it is the reason to prepare properly. The losers, overwhelmingly, are not undone by a lack of cleverness. They are undone by costs they never counted, by risk they never sized, by a volatility spike they never imagined, and by the very human impulses — revenge, hope, the fear of missing out — that override knowledge in the moment that matters. This book is built, from its first page to its last, around *not* trading like them.

The approach is deliberately layered, and the order is not accidental. We begin with the **foundations** — what these contracts are, how they settle, and the transaction costs that quietly govern everything. We build the **pricing** intuition next: intrinsic and time value, the six factors that move a premium, the models that formalise them, and the option chain where all of it becomes visible. We then spend five chapters on **the Greeks**, because Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega, and Rho are the language in which option risk is actually spoken, and portfolio Greeks are how professionals see an entire book at a glance. From there we turn to **volatility** — India VIX, implied versus realised, and the skew — because volatility, not direction, is what options are really about. Only *then* do we reach the **strategies**, from simple directional trades to spreads, condors, calendars, and backspreads, followed by the **practice** of trading them through weekly expiries, scheduled events, and the intraday session. The final two parts are the ones the losers skip: **risk management** — the framework, position sizing, and margin discipline that determine survival — and **the professional's craft** — building a tested system, mastering your own psychology, and running trading as a lawful, solvent business.

Three convictions run through the whole book. The first is that **costs and risk dominate outcomes far more than entry signals do** — so we quantify a round trip, a margin call, and a drawdown as carefully as any strategy. The second is that **an option is a position in volatility and time, not merely a leveraged bet on direction** — so the Greeks and the volatility surface are treated as core skills, not appendices. The third is that **survival is the precondition for every future rupee** — so defined risk, small sizing, and honest self-assessment are presented not as caution but as the professional's edge.

A word on numbers and dates. The Indian derivatives market has changed faster in the last two years than in the two decades before them — lot sizes cut, expiry days standardised, margins tightened, taxes raised. Every market-specific figure in this book — the NIFTY lot of 65, the Tuesday NIFTY expiry and Thursday SENSEX expiry, the 0.15% sell-side STT, the margin frameworks, the tax slabs — is stated as of **2026** and verified against the exchange and SEBI circulars in force at the time of writing. These specifics *will* change again. The **principles** — the arithmetic of the Greeks, the logic of the variance risk premium, the mathematics of drawdown and ruin, the psychology of loss — will not. Wherever a number is time-sensitive, the book says so and tells you to verify the current position before you trade. Treat the specifics as a snapshot and the principles as the lasting lesson.

This is not a book of secrets or a promise of easy income; no honest book about options could be. It is a complete, rigorous, India-specific course in a demanding craft — one that rewards the patient, the disciplined, and the cost-aware, and punishes everyone else. If you read it in order, work the examples with your own calculator, and treat risk management as seriously as opportunity, you will finish it able to think about index options the way a professional does. What you do with that is up to you.

Trade well, and trade to survive.
