# List of Diagrams

This book teaches primarily through **worked numerical tables** (see the List of Tables) and **in-line payoff sketches, schematics, and ASCII charts** rendered at the point of use, rather than through separately numbered plates. The diagrams and illustrations that appear are listed below by chapter, with the concept each conveys. Readers are encouraged to redraw every payoff diagram by hand — the act of plotting max profit, max loss, and the breakeven(s) is one of the fastest ways to internalise a structure.

**Part I — Foundations**

- **Option-symbol breakdown** (Chapter 2) — a labelled schematic decoding a contract symbol (e.g., `NIFTY 31JUL 24500 CE`) into underlying, expiry, strike, and right.

**Part II — Pricing and Valuation**

- **Payoff diagram — Long Call** (Chapter 4) — the classic bought-call "hockey stick": capped loss (premium), unlimited upside, breakeven at strike + premium.
- **Payoff diagram — Long Put** (Chapter 4) — bought put: capped loss, large downside profit, breakeven at strike − premium.
- **Payoff diagram — Short Call** (Chapter 4) — sold call: capped premium gain, unlimited loss.
- **Payoff diagram — Short Put** (Chapter 4) — sold put: capped premium gain, large downside loss.
- **Time-value-vs-moneyness curve** (Chapter 4, described) — time value peaking at the money and falling toward the wings.
- **Call and Put OI profiles** (Chapter 7) — two horizontal ASCII bar charts showing call open-interest as potential resistance and put open-interest as potential support, largest strikes first.

**Part III — The Greeks**

- **Delta-by-moneyness profile** (Chapter 8, tabular/described) — the S-shaped curve from ~0 (deep OTM) through ~0.5 (ATM) to ~1 (deep ITM).
- **Gamma surface** (Chapter 9, tabular/described) — Gamma peaking at the money and exploding near expiry.
- **Theta decay curve** (Chapter 10, described) — the accelerating, non-linear √T decay of time value into expiry.

**Part IV — Volatility**

- **India VIX vs NIFTY** (Chapter 13, described) — the inverse relationship and mean-reverting spikes.
- **IV-vs-HV overlay and the volatility cone** (Chapter 14, described) — implied volatility riding above realised, and the percentile cone narrowing with lookback.
- **Volatility skew and term-structure curves** (Chapter 15, described) — the downward-sloping equity-index skew and the contango/backwardation of ATM IV across expiries.

**Part V — Strategies**

- **Payoff sketches for each structure** (Chapters 16–20, mostly described in prose) — long/short calls and puts; the four vertical spreads; straddles, strangles, iron condors, and butterflies; calendars and diagonals; ratio spreads and backspreads.
- **Calendar-spread payoff "tent"** (Chapter 19) — an ASCII sketch of the calendar's peaked P&L, maximised at the strike near the short leg's expiry.

**Part VI–VIII — Practice, Risk, and the Professional Trader**

- **Intraday remaining-range decay, expiry-day Gamma, drawdown-recovery, risk-of-ruin, and the shrinking-edge funnel** (Chapters 24–28) — presented as numbered tables (see the List of Tables) rather than plotted figures.
- **Conceptual analogies** (Chapters 28–29) — the flight checklist, the throttle-and-engine, the fuel gauge, and the golf-swing (process vs outcome) are teaching metaphors in prose, not rendered figures.

*Note: because the book's illustrations are embedded at the point of use rather than numbered as plates, this list is organised by chapter and concept. Every payoff diagram in Parts II and V is worth reproducing by hand as a study exercise.*
