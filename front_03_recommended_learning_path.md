# Recommended Learning Path

There is no single right way through thirty chapters, because readers arrive with different backgrounds and goals. Below are four routes, a dependency map, and a paced study plan. Whichever route you take, the discipline is the same: **work the numbers yourself, and never let the strategy chapters tempt you past the risk chapters.**

## Choose your route

### Route A — The complete beginner (never traded an option)
Read **every chapter, strictly in order, Chapters 1 → 30.** Do not jump ahead to strategies; they will not make sense — or worse, will half-make sense — without the Greeks and volatility foundation. Spend the most time on Part II (Pricing) and Part III (The Greeks); they are the vocabulary for everything after. Before you place a single real trade, you must have read at least through **Chapter 27 (Margin Management)**. Paper-trade before funding an account.

### Route B — The equity investor who wants to hedge or earn income
You understand markets but not options. Read **Part I (1–3)** for the instrument and its costs, **Part II (4–7)** for pricing, then the *conceptual* passes of **Delta (8), Theta (10), and Vega (11)** — you need these three Greeks more than Gamma and Rho at first. Jump to **Chapter 16 (Directional)** for protective puts, covered calls, and cash-secured puts, and **Chapter 25 (§3.7, hedging an equity portfolio)** for the beta-weighted hedge and the collar. Then read **Chapter 30 (Taxation)**. Come back for the full Greeks and volatility chapters as your ambitions grow.

### Route C — The active trader who already trades options (self-taught)
You can trade but suspect your foundations are patchy — most self-taught traders' are. Skim **Parts I–II** to fill gaps, then read **Part III (Greeks) and Part IV (Volatility) in full** — this is where self-taught traders are weakest and where the real edge lives. Read **Part V (Strategies) and Part VI (Practice)** to systematise what you already do. Then read **Parts VII–VIII slowly and completely.** If you are losing money despite "knowing" strategies, your problem is almost certainly in Parts VII–VIII (risk, sizing, margin, psychology, or the business), not in Part V.

### Route D — The aspiring professional (prop desk, quant, or full-time)
Read the whole book, but treat **Part III (Greeks, esp. Chapter 12 portfolio Greeks), Chapter 14 (variance risk premium), Chapter 15 (skew), Chapter 25 (risk framework), Chapter 26 (sizing/Kelly), Chapter 27 (margin/SPAN), and Chapter 28 (systematic backtesting)** as your core. Master the mathematics, not just the prose. Chapter 30's section on professional paths and certifications (NISM VIII/I/XV, CFA, FRM) and building a verifiable track record is your bridge to an institutional seat.

```
                                        Part V Strategies (16–21) ──► Part VI Practice (22–24)
                                                                        │
                                                                        ▼
                                        Part VII Risk (25–27) ──► Part VIII Professional (28–30)
```

Key single-chapter prerequisites, if you must jump:
- **Any strategy chapter (16–21)** needs Delta (8), Theta (10), and Vega (11) at minimum.
- **Iron condors, calendars, and adjustments (18–20)** lean on Gamma (9) and portfolio Greeks (12).
- **Weekly-expiry and intraday trading (22, 24)** need Gamma (9), Theta (10), and transaction costs (3).
- **Position sizing (26)** and **margin (27)** assume the risk framework (25).
- **Trading as a business (30)** stands alone and can be read at any time — but read it *before* you go full-time.

## A paced study plan (about 12 weeks)

For a reader with a few hours a week, treating this as a serious course:

- **Weeks 1–2 — Foundations & Pricing (Ch 1–7).** Goal: read an option chain fluently; decompose any premium into intrinsic and time value; explain how a future and an option settle in India, and what a round trip costs.
- **Weeks 3–4 — The Greeks (Ch 8–12).** Goal: compute Position Delta, Gamma, Theta, and Vega for a small book; explain the Gamma–Theta trade-off; read a portfolio's Greek "fingerprint."
- **Week 5 — Volatility (Ch 13–15).** Goal: read India VIX and the expected move; use IV Rank; explain the variance risk premium and the put skew.
- **Weeks 6–7 — Strategies (Ch 16–21).** Goal: construct and draw the payoff of every core structure; state each one's max profit, max loss, and breakevens; select a strategy from direction × volatility.
- **Week 8 — Trading in Practice (Ch 22–24).** Goal: understand expiry-day Gamma and pinning; size an event trade; know why frequency is the intraday trader's enemy.
- **Weeks 9–10 — Risk Management (Ch 25–27).** Goal: set per-trade, heat, and drawdown limits; size with fractional Kelly; keep a margin buffer and avoid the peak-margin trap. **Do not rush this fortnight.**
- **Weeks 11–12 — The Professional Trader (Ch 28–30).** Goal: design, backtest, and honestly deflate a system; recognise your own behavioural traps; and set up trading as a compliant, adequately-capitalised business.

After Week 12, revisit the **Final Professional Trader Checklist** before every trading day, and re-read Parts VII–VIII once a quarter. The material that keeps you solvent is the material worth reviewing most often.

## One rule for every route

**Never let your reading run ahead of your risk education into your trading.** It is fine to read strategies before you have finished the risk chapters — but it is not fine to *trade* them. Match real capital to the point where you have genuinely absorbed Parts VII and VIII. The market will still be there when you are ready; your capital will not be if you are not.
