# How to Use This Book

This book is a **structured course**, not a reference to be dipped into at random — at least not the first time through. The chapters build on one another: the Greeks (Part III) assume the pricing intuition of Part II; the strategies (Part V) assume the Greeks and the volatility framework of Part IV; and the risk, sizing, and business chapters (Parts VII–VIII) assume everything before them. If you are new to options, **read it in order.** If you are experienced, use the Recommended Learning Path and the Index to go straight to what you need — but do not skip Parts VII and VIII, which are where most traders' results are actually decided.

## How the book is organised

The thirty chapters fall into eight parts, each a self-contained stage of the journey from "what is an option?" to "how do I run this as a profession?":

- **Part I — Foundations (Chapters 1–3):** the Indian derivatives market and its recent reforms; index options and futures and how they settle; and the trading infrastructure — order types, the bid–ask spread, and the transaction costs that govern everything.
- **Part II — Pricing and Valuation (Chapters 4–7):** intrinsic and time value and moneyness; the six factors that move a premium; the pricing models (Black–Scholes–Merton and the binomial tree); and how to read the option chain.
- **Part III — The Greeks (Chapters 8–12):** Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega and Rho, one chapter at a time, culminating in portfolio (position) Greeks — the professional's single risk dashboard.
- **Part IV — Volatility (Chapters 13–15):** India VIX and the expected move; implied versus historical volatility and the variance risk premium; and the volatility skew and term structure.
- **Part V — Strategies (Chapters 16–21):** directional trades; vertical spreads; non-directional structures (straddles, strangles, condors, butterflies); calendars and diagonals; ratio spreads and backspreads; and how to *select* among them.
- **Part VI — Trading in Practice (Chapters 22–24):** weekly-expiry dynamics; event-based trading around Budgets, RBI, and results; and intraday scalping and day trading.
- **Part VII — Risk Management (Chapters 25–27):** the risk-management framework; position sizing and the Kelly criterion; and margin management and portfolio Greeks in practice.
- **Part VIII — The Professional Trader (Chapters 28–30):** building and backtesting a trading system; trading psychology and behavioural finance; and trading as a business — taxation, compliance, and career paths.

## How each chapter is built

Every chapter follows the **same fourteen-section template**, so you always know where to find what you need:

1. **Learning Objectives** — what you will be able to do by the end.
2. **Introduction** — why the topic matters and how it fits the whole.
3. **Core Concepts** — the main teaching, one idea at a time.
4. **Examples (Real-World)** — the concept in a recognisable situation.
5. **Numerical Examples** — the concept worked out with actual numbers.
6. **Calculations** — the reusable formulas and recipes, collected.
7. **Practical Insights** — how a professional actually applies it.
8. **Common Mistakes** — the traps, so you can avoid them.
9. **Case Study** — an extended, realistic scenario that ties the ideas together.
10. **Chapter Summary** — the chapter in a handful of bullets.
11. **Key Takeaways** — the four or five things to remember above all.
12. **Practice Questions** — ten questions, from concept to judgement.
13. **Detailed Solutions** — worked answers to every question.
14. **Mini Glossary** — the chapter's key terms defined.

Work the **Numerical Examples and Practice Questions with your own calculator.** Options are a quantitative craft; reading the arithmetic is not the same as being able to do it under pressure, and the questions are where understanding is tested.

## Callouts to watch for

Three recurring boxes flag the most important points:

- **Beginner Alert** — a common, costly mistake that beginners make. Read every one; each marks a place where intuition misleads.
- **Professional Insight** — how an experienced trader thinks about the point, often the opposite of the amateur's instinct.
- **Market Note** — an India-specific rule, mechanic, or piece of market structure you must know.

## Conventions used throughout

- **Currency and units.** All figures are in Indian rupees (₹). Option Greeks and premiums are usually quoted **per unit** (per index point of the underlying); to get the rupee figure for one contract, multiply by the **lot size**. Per-unit values do not change with the lot size; per-lot rupee values do.
- **Illustrative baseline.** Unless stated otherwise, examples use a NIFTY level of **≈ 24,600** and the current contract specifications — **NIFTY lot 65**, BANKNIFTY 30, FINNIFTY 60, MIDCPNIFTY 120, SENSEX 20 — so that numbers are comparable across chapters. Specific premiums, IVs, and P&Ls are labelled *illustrative*: they teach the shape of a result, not a live quote.
- **Expiries.** As of 2026, weekly expiries exist only on **NIFTY (NSE, Tuesday)** and **SENSEX (BSE, Thursday)**; BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY, and MIDCPNIFTY are monthly-only.
- **Costs and taxes.** Round-trip transaction costs, STT (0.15% on the sell side from April 2026), GST, and margins are treated as first-class parts of every trade, not afterthoughts.
- **Time-sensitive figures.** Anything that depends on the Finance Act, SEBI circulars, or exchange specifications — tax slabs, STT rates, lot sizes, expiry days, margin percentages — is flagged as time-sensitive. **Verify the current position with SEBI, your exchange, and a qualified CA before acting.** The principles endure; the numbers drift.

## A note on the two hardest parts

If you take only one instruction from this page, take this: **do not skim Part VII (Risk Management) or the risk-and-psychology spine of Part VIII.** The strategies are the part everyone wants; risk, sizing, margin, and psychology are the part that decides who is still trading in five years. Read them as carefully as the strategies — more carefully, if anything. That, more than any signal, is how you avoid being one of the nine in ten.
