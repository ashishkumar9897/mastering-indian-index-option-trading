# Complete Glossary

Terms are defined as used in this book, for the Indian index-options market as of 2026. The chapter of fullest treatment is shown in parentheses; many terms recur throughout.

## A

- **Advance tax** — quarterly pre-payment of income tax (15% / 45% / 75% / 100% cumulative by 15 Jun / Sep / Dec / Mar) required when annual tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 (Ch 30).
- **Anchoring** — the bias of fixating on your entry price ("I'll exit at breakeven") instead of the position's current expectancy (Ch 29).
- **Arbitrage** — locking in a near risk-free profit from the same value priced two ways; the force that keeps prices consistent (Ch 1).
- **At-the-money (ATM)** — a strike at (or nearest to) the current underlying level; time value and Gamma/Vega peak here (Ch 4).
- **Auto-square-off** — a broker's forced closure of intraday (MIS) positions before the close; a backstop, not a risk plan (Ch 24).

## B

- **Backspread** — a spread that buys more options than it sells (e.g., 1×2); long volatility, with a convex tail payoff and a "valley" of loss between the strikes (Ch 20).
- **Backtesting** — testing a system's rules against historical data to estimate its edge; only as good as its data and its honesty about costs and overfitting (Ch 28).
- **Backwardation** — a term structure in which near-dated implied volatility exceeds far-dated; typically a fear signal (Ch 13, 15).
- **Basis** — the futures-minus-spot gap (F − S); reflects cost of carry and converges to zero at expiry (Ch 2).
- **Bear call spread** — sell a lower-strike call, buy a higher-strike call; a bearish/neutral credit spread (Ch 17).
- **Bear put spread** — buy a higher-strike put, sell a lower-strike put; a bearish debit spread (Ch 17).
- **Behavioural (vs informational) losses** — losses caused by failing to execute known-correct actions, not by lack of knowledge; the dominant cause of retail failure (Ch 29).
- **Beta-weighted hedge ratio** — the index-hedge quantity scaled by a portfolio's beta: (portfolio value × beta) ÷ (index level × lot size) (Ch 25).
- **Bid / Ask** — the highest price a buyer will pay / the lowest a seller will accept; you buy at the ask and sell at the bid (Ch 3).
- **Binomial model** — an option-pricing tree using risk-neutral probabilities; converges to Black–Scholes–Merton as steps increase (Ch 6).
- **Black-76** — the futures form of the BSM model; feeding the future F handles dividends and carry automatically (Ch 6).
- **Black–Scholes–Merton (BSM)** — the standard European option-pricing formula, C = S·N(d₁) − K·e^(−rT)·N(d₂) (Ch 6).
- **Breakeven** — the underlying level at which a position's expiry P&L is zero (e.g., a bought call's strike + premium) (Ch 4).
- **Brokerage** — the broker's fee per order, typically flat for discount brokers (Ch 3).
- **Broken-wing butterfly** — an asymmetric butterfly that skews the payoff for a directional bias (Ch 18).
- **Bull call spread** — buy a lower-strike call, sell a higher-strike call; a bullish debit spread (Ch 17).
- **Bull put spread** — sell a higher-strike put, buy a lower-strike put; a bullish/neutral credit spread (Ch 17).
- **Butterfly (long)** — buy 1 lower, sell 2 middle, buy 1 upper strike; a cheap, high-reward bet on the underlying pinning the middle strike (Ch 18).
- **Butterfly (skew measure)** — [IV(25Δ put) + IV(25Δ call)]/2 − IV(ATM); measures the convexity ("smile") of the volatility curve (Ch 15).
- **Buyer's bleed** — the daily Theta cost that makes "right on direction, wrong on timing" a losing trade for option buyers (Ch 10).

## C

- **Calendar (horizontal / time) spread** — sell a near-term and buy a far-term option at the same strike; positive Theta and positive Vega (Ch 19).
- **Calendar-day vs trading-day decay** — the debate over whether Theta follows calendar or trading time; the market largely prices weekends in by Friday (Ch 10).
- **Call option (CE)** — the right, not obligation, to buy the underlying (or receive its value above the strike) at expiry (Ch 1, 2).
- **Call writing** — selling calls (often at resistance strikes) for premium income; capped reward, large risk (Ch 7).
- **Cash settlement** — settling a contract in rupees against the expiry settlement level, with no delivery of the underlying; the mode of all Indian index derivatives (Ch 1, 2).
- **Cash-secured put** — selling a put while reserving the full notional in cash; an unleveraged income/acquisition strategy (Ch 16).
- **Change in open interest** — the day's net change in OI; positive means positions opened, negative means closed (Ch 7).
- **Charm** — the rate of change of Delta with time (∂Δ/∂t); drives overnight and weekend Delta drift (Ch 12).
- **Christmas tree / ladder** — a multi-strike extension of the ratio idea (e.g., 1×1×1) that reshapes the payoff (Ch 20).
- **Circuit-breaker (personal)** — a pre-set drawdown or loss limit that halts your trading before a bad period becomes catastrophic (Ch 25).
- **Clearing corporation** — the entity (e.g., NSE Clearing, ICCL) that guarantees settlement by becoming counterparty to every trade (Ch 1).
- **Close-to-close HV** — historical volatility computed from closing prices only (Ch 14).
- **Collar** — a protective put financed by selling an OTM call; near-free downside protection in exchange for a capped upside (Ch 25).
- **Combined margin** — for option sellers, SPAN margin + exposure margin (buyers pay only the premium margin) (Ch 3, 27).
- **Confirmation bias** — seeking information that supports your position and dismissing what contradicts it (Ch 29).
- **Contango** — a term structure in which far-dated implied volatility (or the future) exceeds near-dated; the normal, calm-market shape (Ch 13, 15).
- **Cooling-off rule** — a pre-set pause (e.g., 30 minutes) after a loss, to break the revenge-trading impulse (Ch 24, 29).
- **Correlation risk** — the exposure of all NIFTY positions to the same underlying, which makes apparent diversification illusory (Ch 25).
- **Cost of carry** — the net financing cost minus dividends of holding to expiry; sets a future's fair premium over spot (Ch 1, 2).
- **Counterparty risk** — the risk that the other side of a trade fails to meet its obligation; removed on-exchange by novation (Ch 1).
- **Covered call (call overwriting)** — selling a call against long underlying exposure for income, capping the upside (Ch 16).
- **CPR (Central Pivot Range)** — a pivot-based intraday band; a wide CPR suggests a range day, a narrow one a trending day (Ch 24).
- **Crash-risk premium** — the extra implied volatility charged on OTM puts to compensate for fat-tailed downside risk (Ch 15).
- **Credit spread** — a spread entered for a net credit (bull put, bear call); high probability, defined risk (Ch 17).
- **CVaR (Conditional VaR / Expected Shortfall)** — the average loss on the worst days beyond the VaR threshold; captures the tail that VaR ignores (Ch 25).

## D

- **d₁, d₂** — the auxiliary terms in BSM; d₁ underlies the hedge ratio (Delta), d₂ the risk-neutral probability of finishing ITM (Ch 6).
- **Daily loss limit** — a pre-set loss that ends trading for the day when hit; the intraday trader's single most important rule (Ch 24, 29).
- **Dealer delta-hedging** — market makers trading the underlying to stay delta-neutral; the mechanism behind expiry pinning and amplification (Ch 22).
- **Debit spread** — a spread paid for with a net debit (bull call, bear put); capped cost and capped profit (Ch 17).
- **Decision matrix** — a mapping of directional view × volatility view to the fitting strategies (Ch 21).
- **Deflated Sharpe** — the raw Sharpe ratio discounted for the number of variants tested, sample length, and non-normality; the believable figure (Ch 28).
- **Defined-risk strategy** — a structure with a known maximum loss (spreads, condors, butterflies); margin equals the cap and no gap can exceed it (Ch 25).
- **Delta (Δ)** — the change in an option's premium per one-point move in the underlying; its directional exposure and hedge ratio (Ch 8).
- **Delta-equivalent notional** — Position Delta × index level; directional exposure expressed in rupees (Ch 8).
- **Delta-neutral** — a position with zero net Delta; flat for an instant, requiring continuous re-hedging as Delta drifts (Ch 8).
- **Delta-to-premium ratio** — Delta ÷ premium; the directional leverage per rupee (high = leveraged, low-probability) (Ch 16).
- **Derivative** — a contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset, rate, or index (Ch 1).
- **Diagonal spread** — a calendar with different strikes as well as expiries; adds a directional tilt (Ch 19).
- **Differential IV crush** — the near-term event premium collapsing more than the far-term's after an event; the event-calendar edge (Ch 19, 23).
- **Dispersion trading** — trading index volatility against constituent volatility; effectively a bet on correlation (Ch 15).
- **Dividends (q)** — expected index dividends; a minor pricing factor that lowers calls and raises puts (Ch 5).
- **Double calendar** — calendars placed on both a call and a put strike, widening the profit tent (Ch 19).
- **Drawdown (maximum)** — the largest peak-to-trough fall in capital; recovery requires a disproportionately larger gain (Ch 25).
- **Dynamic hedging** — continuously re-adjusting a hedge as Delta drifts with the underlying and time (Ch 9, 12).

## E

- **Edge** — a positive expected value per trade, after costs, that persists over a large sample; structural or behavioural, not a curve-fit (Ch 28).
- **Effective vs marginal tax rate** — effective = total tax ÷ total income; marginal = the rate on the next rupee (use the marginal rate to provision tax on incremental trading profit) (Ch 30).
- **European-style** — exercisable only at expiry; the style of all Indian index options (Ch 2).
- **Event calendar** — the schedule of market-moving events (Budget, RBI policy, elections, results, global triggers) (Ch 23).
- **Exchange transaction charges** — the exchange's fee on premium value (~0.035% for NSE options) (Ch 3).
- **Exchange-traded derivative** — a standardised, centrally-cleared contract traded on an exchange (Ch 1).
- **Expected move** — the ±range implied by volatility over a horizon: Index × (VIX/100) × √(time); one standard deviation (Ch 13, 23).
- **Expectancy** — (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss); the average profit per trade, which must be positive after costs (Ch 4, 21, 28).
- **Expiry** — the date a contract ceases to exist and is settled (Ch 1).
- **Expiry-day Gamma explosion** — the sharp rise in ATM Gamma as expiry nears, so that Delta flips on tiny moves (Ch 9, 22).
- **Exposure margin** — an additional buffer margin charged on top of SPAN (Ch 3, 27).

## F

- **F&O** — the exchange-traded futures-and-options segment (Ch 1).
- **Fat tails (kurtosis)** — real index returns having far more extreme moves than a normal distribution predicts; the option seller's defining danger (Ch 6, 25).
- **Fixed-percentage model** — risking a constant percentage of current equity per trade; auto-compounds in gains and auto-de-risks in drawdowns (Ch 26).
- **Fixed-rupee model** — risking a constant rupee amount per trade regardless of account size; simple but non-adaptive (Ch 26).
- **F&O turnover** — for tax, the absolute sum of profits and losses across trades (losses add); the trigger for the tax-audit test, not the tax due (Ch 30).
- **Four-factor model** — the strategy-selection framework of directional view, volatility view, time horizon, and risk appetite (Ch 21).
- **Fractional Kelly** — using a fraction (½ or ¼) of the full Kelly bet to cut drawdowns and guard against edge overestimation (Ch 26).
- **Future** — a standardised, symmetric obligation on both parties to settle the index difference at expiry; marked to market daily (Ch 1, 2).

## G

- **Gambler's fallacy** — believing an independent outcome is "due" after a streak (Ch 29).
- **Gamma (Γ)** — the rate of change of Delta per one-point move; the second derivative of price with respect to the underlying (Ch 9).
- **Gamma exposure (GEX)** — aggregate dealer gamma; positive GEX tends to pin the index, negative GEX to amplify moves (Ch 22).
- **Gamma scalping** — a long-Gamma, delta-neutral trader re-hedging (selling rallies, buying dips) to profit from realised movement (Ch 9).
- **Gamma–Theta trade-off** — the inescapable link that long Gamma costs Theta and collecting Theta means short Gamma; both peak ATM near expiry (Ch 9).
- **Geometric mean return** — the compounding growth rate; reduced by volatility (variance drag) and maximised by Kelly (Ch 26, 29).
- **Greek reconciliation** — comparing your broker's displayed Greeks with your own to catch IV-input or model differences (Ch 27).
- **GST** — 18% goods-and-services tax on brokerage, exchange charges, and SEBI fees (not on STT or stamp duty); a cost, with no input credit for traders (Ch 3, 30).

## H

- **Hedge ratio** — Delta expressed as the units of underlying needed to replicate or hedge an option (Ch 8).
- **Hedging** — reducing an existing risk by taking an offsetting derivative position (Ch 1).
- **Hedging priority** — the practical order for adjusting a book: Delta first (with futures), then Vega (with options), then Gamma (by sizing) (Ch 12).
- **Historical (realised) volatility (HV)** — the annualised standard deviation of the underlying's past returns; the reality check on implied volatility (Ch 14).

## I

- **Impact cost** — how far an execution sits from the mid price, as a percentage; rises sharply for illiquid strikes (Ch 3).
- **Implied volatility (IV)** — the volatility embedded in an option's market price; the σ that makes the model match the quote (Ch 5, 6).
- **India VIX** — the NSE's model-free index of expected 30-day annualised NIFTY volatility; the market's "fear gauge" (Ch 1, 13).
- **India VIX futures** — volatility futures launched in 2014 and discontinued in 2017 for lack of liquidity; not currently tradable (Ch 13).
- **Index future** — a both-sided obligation to settle the index difference at expiry, marked to market daily (Ch 2).
- **Index option** — a standardised, exchange-traded, index-based option; a call (CE) or put (PE) (Ch 2).
- **In-the-money (ITM)** — an option with intrinsic value (spot above a call's strike, or below a put's) (Ch 4).
- **Intrinsic value** — the in-the-money amount: max(0, S − K) for a call, max(0, K − S) for a put (Ch 2, 4).
- **Intraday options trading** — opening and closing positions within a single session, squared off before the close (Ch 24).
- **Iron butterfly** — a short ATM straddle with protective wings; a defined-risk short straddle (Ch 18).
- **Iron condor** — a bull put spread plus a bear call spread; a defined-risk short strangle, margined on one side (Ch 17, 18).
- **ITR-3** — the income-tax return form for individuals with business income, including F&O (Ch 30).
- **IV crush** — the sharp fall in implied volatility after an event resolves, deflating option premiums (Ch 5, 11, 23).
- **IV cycle** — the arc of implied volatility around a scheduled event: build-up → peak → crush → normalise (Ch 23).
- **IV Percentile** — the share of past days (over a year) on which IV was below its current level (Ch 14).
- **IV Rank** — where current IV sits in its 52-week range: (IV − low) ÷ (high − low) × 100 (Ch 14).

## K

- **Kelly Criterion** — the growth-optimal bet fraction, f* = (p·b − q) ÷ b; maximises geometric growth but is too aggressive to use in full (Ch 21, 26).

## L

- **Legging risk** — the risk that the market moves before all legs of a multi-leg trade are filled (Ch 3).
- **Leverage** — controlling a large notional with a small outlay; magnifies both gains and losses (Ch 1).
- **Limit order** — an order that executes only at a specified price or better (Ch 3).
- **Liquidity** — the ease of trading at a fair price; concentrated in NIFTY/SENSEX, at the money, and near expiry (Ch 2, 3).
- **Long Gamma** — a (typically bought-option) position whose Delta moves favourably as the underlying moves (Ch 9).
- **Long / short Vega** — a position that gains when IV rises (buyers) / falls (sellers) (Ch 11).
- **Loss aversion** — feeling the pain of a loss about 2–2.5× the pleasure of an equal gain; drives holding losers and cutting winners (Ch 29).
- **Loss carry-forward** — carrying an unabsorbed business loss forward eight assessment years against future business income; requires filing the return on time (Ch 30).
- **Loss set-off** — using a loss to reduce other income; a non-speculative F&O loss offsets any head except salary (Ch 30).
- **Lot size** — the fixed number of index units per contract, set by the exchange (NIFTY 65, BANKNIFTY 30, FINNIFTY 60, MIDCPNIFTY 120, SENSEX 20 as of 2026) (Ch 1, 2).
- **Lot granularity** — the whole-lot trading constraint that prevents exact Delta-neutrality and forces coarse sizing on small accounts (Ch 8, 26).
- **LTP (Last Traded Price)** — the premium of the most recent trade at a strike; can be stale in illiquid strikes (Ch 7).

## M

- **Margin** — collateral a risk-carrying participant (especially a seller) must post (Ch 1).
- **Margin buffer** — the unused margin (~30–40%) kept free to absorb an intraday requirement increase and MTM swing (Ch 27).
- **Margin efficiency** — return per rupee of margin, annualised: (P&L ÷ margin) × (365 ÷ holding days); the true measure for income strategies (Ch 27).
- **Margin shortfall penalty** — 0.5%–5% of the shortfall per day, rising with size and repetition (Ch 27).
- **Mark to market (MTM)** — the daily revaluation (and cash settlement, for futures) of open positions (Ch 1, 2).
- **Market order** — an order that executes immediately at the best available price (Ch 3).
- **Master equation** — the P&L decomposition ΔP ≈ Δ·ΔS + ½·Γ·(ΔS)² + Θ·Δt + ν·Δσ (Ch 12).
- **Matching engine** — the exchange system that pairs orders by price–time priority (Ch 3).
- **Max Pain** — the expiry settlement level that minimises total payout to option holders; a tendency, not a prediction (Ch 7).
- **Maximum loss / maximum profit** — the worst and best outcomes of a position; asymmetric between buyers and sellers (Ch 4).
- **Mean reversion (of volatility)** — the tendency of VIX to return toward its long-run average; spikes and crushes fade (Ch 13).
- **MFE / MAE** — Maximum Favourable / Adverse Excursion: the best / worst unrealised P&L a trade reached (Ch 22).
- **Mid** — the average of the bid and ask; the reference "fair" price you rarely actually trade at (Ch 3).
- **Minimum edge** — the premium move needed to cover the round-trip cost (~₹0.96/unit at ~₹62.5/round trip, lot 65) (Ch 24).
- **Model-free implied volatility** — volatility derived from a whole strip of options (as India VIX is), not a single-option model (Ch 13).
- **Moneyness** — a strike's position relative to spot: in-, at-, or out-of-the-money (Ch 2, 4).
- **Multiple-testing (data-mining) bias** — the inflation of the best result when many strategy variants are tested; p-hacking (Ch 28).

## N

- **N(·)** — the standard normal cumulative distribution function; converts BSM's d-values into probabilities (Ch 6).
- **Naked leg / naked option** — an uncovered short option; source of open-ended risk and heavy margin (Ch 16, 20).
- **NIFTY 50** — India's benchmark equity index of 50 large, liquid companies; the underlying for NIFTY options (Ch 1).
- **NISM Series VIII / I / XV** — the Equity Derivatives / Currency Derivatives / Research Analyst certifications for Indian derivatives professionals (Ch 30).
- **No-arbitrage** — the principle that prices leave no risk-free profit; the basis of option pricing (Ch 6).
- **Non-directional strategy** — a position that profits from the magnitude (or absence) of movement rather than its direction (Ch 18).
- **Non-speculative business income** — the tax classification of exchange-traded F&O income; filed on ITR-3, with deductible expenses and favourable loss rules (Ch 30).
- **Notional value** — the full face value a contract controls (index level × lot size) (Ch 1).
- **Novation** — the clearing corporation stepping between buyer and seller as counterparty to each, removing counterparty risk (Ch 1).

## O

- **Open interest (OI)** — the number of contracts currently open at a strike; carries over day to day (unlike volume) (Ch 7).
- **OI concentration** — the strikes with the largest OI; heavy call OI marks resistance, heavy put OI marks support (Ch 7).
- **Opening Range Breakout (ORB)** — an intraday entry trigger based on a break of the first 15–30 minutes' high or low (Ch 24).
- **Optionality** — the value of a one-sided right under uncertainty and remaining time (Ch 4).
- **Option chain** — the grid of all listed calls and puts by strike, with prices, volume, OI, and IV (Ch 7).
- **Order flow** — the path of an order from platform through broker to the exchange matching engine and clearing (Ch 3).
- **Order validity (DAY / IOC)** — how long an order stays live: DAY rests until close, IOC fills immediately or cancels (Ch 3).
- **OTC (over-the-counter)** — a private, bilateral derivative that carries counterparty risk (Ch 1).
- **Out-of-the-money (OTM)** — an option with no intrinsic value yet (Ch 1, 4).
- **Overconfidence** — increasing size after a winning streak, mistaking luck for skill (Ch 29).
- **Overnight gap risk** — the un-hedgeable risk that the index gaps far from the close while the market is shut (Ch 23).
- **Overtrading** — taking more trades than edge and discipline justify; the number-one intraday P&L killer (Ch 24).

## P

- **Parkinson HV** — a historical-volatility estimator using the intraday high–low range; more efficient but ignores gaps (Ch 14).
- **Payoff diagram** — a plot of a position's expiry P&L against the underlying level (Ch 4).
- **Peak margin** — the requirement that full margin be available at four random daily snapshots; the highest snapshot sets it (Ch 27).
- **Peak-margin trap** — a transient mid-day margin spike (opening a position before closing another) caught by a random snapshot and penalised (Ch 27).
- **Pin risk (the expiry effect)** — the tendency of the index to settle near a high-open-interest strike at expiry (Ch 22).
- **P&L attribution** — decomposing a period's P&L into Delta, Gamma, Theta, and Vega contributions (Ch 12).
- **Poor man's covered call** — a deep-ITM, long-dated call financed by rolling short near-term calls; a diagonal spread (Ch 19).
- **Portfolio (position) Greeks** — the net Δ / Γ / Θ / ν / ρ of an entire book: Σ(Greek × quantity × lot size) (Ch 12).
- **Portfolio heat** — total risk deployed as a fraction of capital; capped lower than for uncorrelated books because NIFTY positions move together (Ch 25).
- **Portfolio margining** — the netting of offsetting positions' risk, reducing total margin below the sum of the parts (Ch 27).
- **Position Delta / Gamma / Theta / Vega** — the aggregate of each Greek across a position's legs (Ch 8–12).
- **Position sizing** — the decision of how much capital (how many lots) to risk per trade; the top determinant of survival (Ch 26).
- **Pre-open session** — the cash-market call auction (9:00–9:15 IST) that discovers the open; options trade from 9:15 (Ch 3).
- **Premium** — the per-unit price of an option; paid by the buyer, received by the seller (Ch 1, 2).
- **Premium margin** — the full premium collected upfront from an option buyer (Ch 3).
- **Pricing model** — a formula returning an option's fair, no-arbitrage premium from its inputs (Ch 6).
- **Probability of profit (POP)** — the chance a trade finishes profitable; below Delta for buyers, higher for sellers (Ch 16).
- **Process over outcome** — judging a trade by whether the plan was followed, not by whether it won (Ch 28, 29).
- **Product type (MIS / NRML)** — the intraday vs carry-forward order category governing leverage and auto-square-off (Ch 3).
- **Prospect theory** — the behavioural model explaining the asymmetric pain of losses versus gains (Kahneman–Tversky) (Ch 29).
- **Protective put** — buying a put to insure a long position; the strike sets the "deductible" (Ch 16).
- **Protective-put overlay** — buying index puts to insure a portfolio; a steady cost with a convex downside payoff (Ch 25).
- **Put–call parity** — the identity linking calls, puts, and the future at one strike and expiry; for Delta, Δcall − Δput = 1 (Ch 2, 4, 8).
- **Put option (PE)** — the right, not obligation, to sell the underlying (or receive its value below the strike) at expiry (Ch 1, 2).
- **Put–Call Ratio (PCR)** — total put OI (or volume) ÷ total call OI (or volume); most useful at extremes, read contrarian (Ch 7).
- **Put writing** — selling puts (often at support strikes) for premium income (Ch 7).

## R

- **Ratio spread** — a spread that sells more options than it buys (e.g., 1×2); short volatility, with a naked extra leg and open-ended risk (Ch 20).
- **Realised-vs-implied tracking** — checking whether realised volatility matched what was implied; P&L attribution for volatility trades (Ch 14).
- **Recency bias** — assuming the recent market regime (calm or trending) will continue (Ch 29).
- **Recovery asymmetry** — the disproportionate gain needed to recover a drawdown: gain = 1/(1 − DD) − 1 (a 50% loss needs +100%, a 90% loss +900%) (Ch 25).
- **Replication** — reproducing an option's payoff with underlying plus cash; its cost is the fair price (Ch 6).
- **Return on margin** — profit ÷ margin (= max loss for a defined-risk spread); the true efficiency of an income trade (Ch 17).
- **Revenge trading** — trying to recover a loss with larger, riskier trades; a chief cause of blow-ups (Ch 29).
- **Reward-to-risk ratio** — average win ÷ average loss; inversely related to the breakeven win rate, 1/(1 + RR) (Ch 21).
- **Rho (ρ)** — the change in premium per 100 bps change in the interest rate; positive for calls, negative for puts, scaling with time (Ch 5, 11).
- **Risk-neutral pricing** — valuing by discounting expected payoffs in a world where all assets drift at the risk-free rate; a pricing device, not a forecast (Ch 6).
- **Risk of ruin** — the probability of losing enough to be knocked out; falls exponentially as per-trade risk shrinks (Ch 26).
- **Risk reversal** — IV(25Δ put) − IV(25Δ call); measures the slope of the skew (positive = put skew) (Ch 15).
- **R-multiple** — a trade's profit divided by its initial risk (a full stop-out is −1R); a size-independent edge measure (Ch 28).
- **Rolling trap** — repeatedly rolling a losing position to avoid realising the loss, turning one capped loss into many (Ch 17, 18, 25).
- **Rupee (dollar) Gamma** — Γ × S² / 100; the shift in rupee delta-exposure per 1% move in the underlying (Ch 9).

## S

- **Scalping** — capturing tiny moves for small, frequent profits; viable only in tight-spread ATM strikes (Ch 24).
- **Scenario analysis / stress testing** — computing a book's P&L across a grid of adverse moves (e.g., a 10% crash with VIX doubling) before they occur (Ch 12, 25).
- **SEBI** — the Securities and Exchange Board of India, the market regulator (Ch 1).
- **Section 44AB (tax audit)** — the requirement to have accounts audited by a CA above the turnover threshold (practically ₹10 crore for digital-only F&O) (Ch 30).
- **Section 44AD** — the presumptive-taxation scheme; its applicability to F&O is contested and interacts with the audit rules (Ch 30).
- **Seller's identity** — the recurring seller's Greek signature: short Gamma ≈ long Theta ≈ short Vega (Ch 12).
- **Session dynamics** — the three phases of the trading day: the volatile open, the tradable mid-session, and the squaring close (Ch 24).
- **Settlement price** — the specified underlying value at expiry that a contract settles against (Ch 2).
- **Sharpe ratio** — (mean return − risk-free rate) ÷ standard deviation of returns; risk-adjusted return per unit of total volatility (Ch 28).
- **Skew (volatility skew)** — the variation of IV across strikes; downward-sloping for equity indices (OTM puts richest) (Ch 6, 7, 15).
- **Skew-adjusted (minimum-variance) delta** — the true directional exposure including skew co-movement; more negative than BSM Delta for index puts (Ch 15).
- **Slippage** — the gap between the expected and actual fill price; compounds across legs (Ch 3).
- **Sortino ratio** — like the Sharpe but dividing by downside deviation only; more relevant for asymmetric-return strategies (Ch 28).
- **SPAN margin** — the margin from the worst-case loss across a grid of price × volatility scenarios (Ch 3, 27).
- **Speculation** — voluntarily taking on risk to profit from a market view (Ch 1).
- **Spread (bid–ask)** — the gap between the bid and ask; a hidden cost crossed on both entry and exit (Ch 3).
- **Spread margining** — the collapse in margin from adding a protective long to a naked short (converting it into a spread) (Ch 27).
- **Spread width** — the distance between a spread's strikes; sets its maximum risk and credit (Ch 17).
- **Square-root-of-time relationship** — time value scales with √T, so Theta and Gamma scale with 1/√T and Vega with √T (Ch 5, 10).
- **Stamp duty** — a state levy on the buy side of a trade (~0.003% of premium) (Ch 3).
- **Sticky strike / sticky delta** — models of how the skew behaves as spot moves: each strike's IV stays fixed (sticky strike), or each delta's IV stays fixed and the skew slides with spot (sticky delta) (Ch 15).
- **Stop-loss order (SL / SL-M)** — an order that becomes a limit (SL) or market (SL-M) order once a trigger price is hit (Ch 3).
- **STT (Securities Transaction Tax)** — a tax on securities transactions; from 1 April 2026, 0.15% on option sale (premium) and on exercise (intrinsic value), and 0.05% on futures sale (Ch 2, 3).
- **Strategy rotation** — shifting the active strategy set as the market regime changes (Ch 21).
- **Streak probability** — the (loss rate)^k chance of k consecutive losses; shows losing streaks are a normal feature of a winning strategy (Ch 29).
- **Strike price (K)** — the fixed level at which an option settles or exercises (Ch 1, 2, 5).
- **Sunk cost fallacy** — escalating a losing trade because of what is already lost, ignoring future expectancy (Ch 29).
- **Synthetic future** — a futures-equivalent built from options: Strike + (Call − Put) (Ch 2, 4, 7).

## T

- **Tail risk** — the danger of fat-tailed extreme moves, especially severe for leveraged option sellers (Ch 25).
- **Term structure (of volatility)** — how implied volatility differs across expiries; contango (far > near) or backwardation (near > far) (Ch 13, 15, 19).
- **Theta (Θ)** — the daily loss of an option's value from the passage of time; negative for long options, positive for short (Ch 10).
- **Theta as % of premium** — Θ ÷ premium; the decay rate relative to price and a proxy for Gamma risk (Ch 10).
- **The 30-day rule** — the observation that most of an option's value decays in its final 30 days, and much of that in the last week (Ch 10).
- **Three-year-runway rule** — holding at least three years of living expenses in safe, non-trading assets before trading full-time (Ch 30).
- **Tick size** — the smallest permitted premium increment (₹0.05 for index options) (Ch 2).
- **Tilt** — trading emotionally (especially after a loss) with impaired judgement (Ch 29).
- **Time decay** — the erosion of time value as expiry approaches; non-linear and accelerating (Ch 5, 10).
- **Time stop** — exiting a long option by a set date if the thesis has not played out, to limit Theta bleed (Ch 16).
- **Time value** — premium minus intrinsic value; the price of remaining time and uncertainty (Ch 2, 4).
- **Trade playbook / SOP** — the one-card-per-strategy specification and the daily pre-/during-/post-market routine that operationalise a system (Ch 28).
- **Trading system** — a completely codified set of rules (entry, exit, sizing, adjustments, risk) that removes in-the-moment judgement (Ch 28).
- **Tracking (basis) risk** — the residual risk when an index hedge does not perfectly offset a non-index portfolio (Ch 25).
- **Turnover (notional vs premium)** — total contract face value traded vs total premium actually exchanged (Ch 1); distinct from the tax definition of F&O turnover (Ch 30).

## U

- **Undefined-risk strategy** — a structure with open-ended loss (naked options, short strangles), bounded only by margin and a cushion (Ch 25).
- **Underlying** — the asset, rate, or index a derivative is based on; for index options, in practice the index future (Ch 1, 5).

## V

- **Vanna** — the Delta–volatility cross-Greek (∂Δ/∂σ = ∂ν/∂S); Delta shifts when IV moves (Ch 12).
- **VaR (Value at Risk)** — the loss a portfolio will not exceed on 95% (say) of days; understates option tail risk (Ch 25).
- **Variance drag** — the reduction in compounding growth caused by volatility, for a given average return (Ch 29).
- **Variance risk premium (VRP)** — the tendency of implied to exceed realised volatility (~80% of the time); the structural edge of option selling (Ch 14).
- **Vega (ν)** — the change in premium per one-point (one-vol-point) change in implied volatility; positive for all long options (Ch 11).
- **Vega-neutral** — a position with zero net Vega, used to isolate other Greeks (Ch 11).
- **Vertical spread** — a two-leg position of the same type and expiry but different strikes; defines risk and reduces margin (Ch 17).
- **VIX percentile** — where current VIX sits within its own recent distribution (Ch 13).
- **Volatility (σ)** — the expected annualised movement of the underlying; the market's estimate is implied volatility (Ch 5).
- **Volatility cone** — historical percentile bands of realised volatility by lookback window, narrowing as the window lengthens (Ch 14).
- **Volatility regime** — the band VIX occupies (e.g., low 10–13, normal 13–18, high 18–25, crisis 25+) (Ch 13).
- **Volatility smile** — a symmetric skew (both wings high), typical of currencies (Ch 15).
- **Volga (Vomma)** — the rate of change of Vega with IV (∂ν/∂σ); makes short-Vega losses accelerate in a spike (Ch 12).
- **Vol point** — one percentage point of implied volatility (e.g., IV 13% → 14%) (Ch 11).
- **Volume** — the number of contracts traded at a strike today; resets daily (Ch 7).
- **VWAP** — Volume Weighted Average Price; the day's fair-value line, used as an intraday trend filter and support/resistance (Ch 24).

## W

- **Walk-forward analysis** — optimising a system on an in-sample period and testing it, unchanged, on a separate out-of-sample period (Ch 28).
- **Weekly expiry** — options expiring each week; in India, only NIFTY (NSE, Tuesday) and SENSEX (BSE, Thursday) as of 2026 (Ch 22).
- **Weekend Theta** — decay attributed to non-trading days; in practice largely pre-priced into Friday's premiums (Ch 10).
- **Wing width** — the distance between the short and long strikes of a condor's or butterfly's spread; sets its maximum loss (Ch 18).

## Y

- **Yang–Zhang HV** — the most complete historical-volatility estimator, using open-high-low-close data and overnight gaps (Ch 14).

## Z

- **Zero-sum (and negative-sum after costs)** — a market in which one side's gain equals the other's loss; derivatives are zero-sum before costs and negative-sum after them (Ch 1).
- **0 DTE** — trading options on their expiry day (zero days to expiry), where Gamma is at its most extreme (Ch 22).
